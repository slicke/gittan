# Application icon

Read this when touching the artwork, `make icons`, the .ico/.icns/PNGs or how the macOS bundle gets its icon.

- The artwork is two finished square PNGs, both cut once out of 1024-pixel pictures on a dark background; a new artwork must likewise be cut out before `make icons`. `icons/gittan-source.png` (646 pixels) is the motif alone, a document of white rope with a red and a green strand under a magnifying glass, transparent around it: the background was modeled as a smooth gradient and taken away, the drop shadow under the motif with it, and the magnifier's grip, whose dark band and underside are close to the background's color, added as a capsule fitted to its edges. `icons/gittan-source-mac.png` (768 pixels) is the same motif on a dark tile with a glass rim, transparent outside the tile; the tile's corners are not a superellipse (the bottom ones are squarer than the top), so it was clipped along the rim's outer edge traced from the picture, 1.5 pixels inside it so none of the background shows. The tile's face inside the rim was then darkened (red, green and blue scaled by 0.62, 0.58 and 0.56, the motif left as it was) from slate blue to nearly black, about #1e2229 in the middle. `make icons` runs `tools/makeicons.swift` (macOS only: CoreGraphics and `iconutil`). Windows and Linux (`Gittan.ico`, `icons/gittan.png` at 1024 and `icons/png/gittan-<n>.png` at 16 to 512 for desktop files) get the motif filling its canvas. macOS (`icons/Gittan.icns`) gets the tile inset to Apple's 824/1024 grid over a soft drop shadow, so it sits like other dock icons. Commit the generated files; other platforms only consume them.
- `Gittan.ico` beside `Gittan.lpi` is the project icon (`<Icon Value="0"/>` in the .lpi means it is set); Lazarus compiles it into the executable's resources, which the LCL uses for the window and taskbar icon on Windows and Linux. The .icns insets the tile to Apple's 824/1024 grid; the .ico and PNGs have the motif without a tile, filling their canvas.
- Lazarus creates `Gittan.app` without an icon, so the Darwin build targets in the Makefile copy `icons/Gittan.icns` into `Contents/Resources` and add `CFBundleIconFile` to `Info.plist` after lazbuild. The `Info.plist` lazbuild writes into a new bundle has the project title as `CFBundleName` with a bare `&`, which PlistBuddy refuses to read (so CI, which always starts without a bundle, shipped no icon); the targets escape it, set `CFBundleName` to Gittan and check the result with `plutil -lint`. A bundle built from the Lazarus IDE lacks the icon until the next `make`. Finder caches icons; `touch Gittan.app` or a relaunch of Finder refreshes it.
