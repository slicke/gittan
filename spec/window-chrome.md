# Window chrome: file header, status bar, banner, title bar, change navigator

Read this when touching `gittan.fileheader`, `gittan.statusbar`, `gittan.banner`, `gittan.changenav`, the macOS toolbar, the Windows/GTK title bars or the minimap.

- `gittan.fileheader.pp` holds `TGittanFileHeader`, a self-painted bar above the editor (`alTop` in `EditorPanel`, the panel from `umain.lfm` that holds the editor and the summary's scroll box): the file's badge from `TreeImages`, its directory dimmed and its name in full color, then on the right the added and deleted counts (or "binary"), the lexer name, the action pills and the change navigator, which it owns as a child. `ShowChanges` fills it from `FStats` (the stats of the last `LoadRepository`), `ShowRepoDiff` titles it "Repository diff" with the totals, `OpenFile` shows the path alone; `UpdateStatus` sets the lexer name. `ShowEditor` shows it and `ShowSummary` hides it. `ApplyTheme` colors it like the summary, on the gutter's shade, and gives the pills the navigator's colors (`SetPillColors`). The pills (`SetActions`, `SetActionEnabled`, `OnAction` with the index) are painted in `Paint` and hit-tested in the mouse handlers as the navigator's arrows are; `SetModified` draws a dot after the file name and `SetNote` a warning in the deleted color left of the pills. `SetFile` and `SetTitle` clear the pills, the dot and the note, so a view sets them after its title. Left of the toggle sits the View chip (`SetMenu`, a pill with a chevron that stays through `SetFile` and `SetTitle` and in the header on macOS too, `OnMenu` with its screen rectangle): `HeaderMenu` in `umain.actions.inc` drops `FViewPopup` from it with the View menu's modes (Hide Unchanged Lines, Side by Side, Word Wrap, Ignore Whitespace, Blame, Translations, Review Comments) checked as there, then Code History and File History from the Go menu, each with its shortcut (`ShortcutLabel`: ⌃⌥⇧⌘ before the key on macOS, `ShortCutToText` elsewhere), only the items the menus enable now; a pick clicks the menu item (`FViewItems`, `FViewRows`), so the chip and the menus cannot drift apart.
- `gittan.statusbar.pp` holds `TGittanStatusBar`, the self-painted status bar (`FStatus`, `alBottom` on the frame, created in `Init` before anything calls `UpdateChangeNav`), in the file header's palette from `ApplyTheme`, since the native `TStatusBar` kept its light look on a dark window. It is laid out as a music player's bar, 56 points high (`BarHeight`), in three zones. On the left where you are: a tile with the repository's initial on a tint of the accent (`Initial`, the name's first UTF-8 character; a plain file's tile takes the file name's), the name in bold over the branch as a chip with a branch icon and a chevron, the base label ("vs origin/main", `SetBase`, clickable when it has actions) dimmed after the branch, and the stashes as a chip with a box and their count (`SetStashes`, hidden with none); the tile and the name share the click (`spRepo`, back to the summary; its rectangle grows over the name and so spans the branch's row too, and `PartAt` tests the branch and the base before it). In the middle the transport, as a player's: a round button on each side of the primary one, a pill that says the next step (`SetTransport` with three `TGittanStatusButton`s, made by `StatusButton`: a caption, a `Hint*` icon, a tone and whether it is enabled; the side buttons show their icon alone and their caption is their hint). `UpdateStatusTransport` in `umain.pp` decides them and keeps what each does in `FStatusSteps` (`TStatusStep`), which `StatusStepClick` runs: while git is in the middle of something (not bisecting) Abort (a cross, `AbortOperationClick`), then Resolve with the conflict count in red (`HintWarn`; it drops the conflicted files as the old badge did, `ConflictBadgeClick`), Commit Merge for a merge without conflicts (`CommitButtonClick`) or Continue (`HintPlay`, `ContinueOperationClick`, enabled in `ContinueMode`), and Skip (`HintSkip`, `SkipOperationClick`) where the operation can skip; otherwise Fetch (`HintFetch`, two arcs), then Pull in the accent with commits to pull (also when both sides moved on, the hint says so), Force Push instead (`stepForcePush`, `ForcePushAsk`, with Pull beside it) when both moved on because the branch was rewritten here (`FRewritten`, the snapshot's `Rewritten`: the upstream's tip is reached from one of the branch's newest reflog entries, `TGittanRepo.UpstreamInReflog`, as `push --force-if-includes` asks; tested in `TestUpstreamInReflog`), since a pull after a rebase or amend of pushed commits would bring the replaced ones back (a rejected push then offers Force Push first too), Push with commits to push or without an upstream (the hint says the branch is new on the remote), or Up to date in the hover fill with a green check and no click (`stGood`), and Push or Pull as the other side button, disabled when it would do nothing; all rest while a remote job or a commit runs. Under the transport the track: a thin line with "↓5" (behind) on its left and "↑2" (ahead) on its right (`SetSync`; the counts are dimmed at 0 and the whole row is `spSync`, which drops the sync list with pull, push, force push, fetch and the commit graph, `SyncClick`); the track carries instead, in this order, a passing message (`ShowMessage`, in its tone), the progress of a long job (`SetProgress`, the line filled in the accent: the indexing) or what git is in the middle of (`SetOperation`: "Merging", or "Rebasing · 3 of 7" from `TGittanRepo.OperationProgress`, which reads msgnum and end in rebase-merge, next and last in rebase-apply, tested in `TestOperation`; in amber, the line filled by the step). On the right, from the right edge: the changed files (`SetFiles`, back to the summary), the caret's position, the pull request (a pull request icon, "#12", the state from `PullRequestNote` dimmed, the checks as an icon in their tone with the failing or pending count after it: `PullChecks` in `umain.actions.inc` gives red with the failing ones, amber with the pending ones, green when all passed, nothing without checks; then the review threads as a speech bubble with the open count) and a newer Gittan as a green badge ("Build 109 available", `SetUpdate`). Every part is measured first with everything shown (`LeftWidth`, `MidWidth`, `RightWidth` in `Paint`); while they do not fit the window the parts give way one step at a time from the outside in: the caret's position, the pull request's state, the file count, the stashes and the base, the name (the tile stays), the track narrows (`TrackNarrow`), the pull request goes, the track goes, the update badge goes; a message is then cut to what is left. The transport and the track are centered in the window while the zones leave room, else placed between them. Every part (`TGittanStatusPart`) has its own hint (`PartHint`; the message's is its full text), which the bar shows itself in a `TGittanStatusHint` (a `THintWindow` painted in the bar's palette) above the part after `Application.HintPause`; a hint line that starts with `HintPassed`, `HintFailed`, `HintPending`, `HintLink`, `HintSwitch`, `HintFolder`, `HintBranch`, `HintPull`, `HintComment`, `HintSync`, `HintStash`, `HintFetch`, `HintDown`, `HintUp`, `HintPlay`, `HintSkip` or `HintWarn` and a space gets that marker drawn as an icon (a green check, a red cross, an amber clock, a globe, two opposed arrows, a folder, a branch, a pull request, a speech bubble, an arrow up beside one down, a box, two arcs round a circle, an arrow down, an arrow up, a triangle, a triangle at a bar, a warning triangle). `DrawStatusIcon` draws them with canvas lines, for the hint, for the icons in the bar itself and for the other views that borrow them, and a hint of several lines has its first line bold. `PullHint` uses them for the pull request: "#1 title", one line per outcome of the checks, worst first, and "Click to compare with it or open it on GitHub" (the LCL's own hint, which follows controls, never came back once the mouse had moved within the bar on Cocoa, so `ShowHint` is off). The clickable parts (`StatusClickable`, a transport button only while enabled, the base only with actions: `Clickable`) get a hand cursor, a fill under the mouse (the primary button brightens, a side button gets a filled circle) and `OnPartClick` with the part's screen rectangle: `StatusPartClick` drops the branch list up from the branch chip (the summary's `SummaryBranchClick`), drops the sync list from the track, drops a list from the pull request (`PullChipClick`, `FPullPopup`: Compare With This Pull Request, with the open review threads as its detail, which fetches and compares it through `ComparePull` as Compare With > Pull Requests does; Compare With the Working Tree; Open on GitHub; Copy Link), lists the stashes from their chip, and goes back to the summary from the tile, the name or the file count. The right click menu (`FStatusPopup`) copies the branch name or the repository path.
- `gittan.banner.pp` holds `TGittanBanner` (`FBanner`), a self-painted strip at the top of `EditorPanel`, over the file header or the summary, for what failed: git's refusals (switch, stash, cherry-pick and the other branch and commit actions, revert, a commit), which were modal `MessageDlg`s, and every `StatusNote` with `AError`, which was a red message in the status bar for `StatusErrorSeconds` (its first line the title, the rest the detail). `BannerError` shows a title in bold with the first line of the detail dimmed after it, in a red tint, until the close button takes it away; `BannerInfo` a note in the accent's tint that closes itself after `BannerInfoSeconds` (not while the mouse is on it; a manual update check that finds Gittan current), and `BannerSuccess` the same in green with a check: a push that went through ("Pushed 2 commits on main", from `FRemoteAhead`) and a pull ("Pulled 3 commits into main", or that the branch was up to date: `TGittanRepo.CountCommits` from `FRemoteHead`, HEAD's `CommitId` when the pull started, to `@{upstream}`, which counts what came in also when a rebase gave the local commits new ids; both tested), while a fetch, a commit that cannot be pushed as it is and a deleted remote branch keep their note in the status bar, being frequent or plain from the tree. `AddAction` adds a message with an action, a link in bold by the close button that closes the message and calls its handler (Push after a commit, see the Commit section in commit-and-remote.md); such a message offers no Copy. Details (when the detail has more lines or is cut) unfolds it in the editor's font, `MaxLines` at most, and Copy puts the title and the whole detail on the clipboard (not offered for a note or a success that is its title alone). A new message goes over the one in view, the same one is not added twice, and "+2" before the close button counts those under it, which closing brings back; `OpenRepository` clears them. `OnChange` (shown, hidden or resized) runs `PlaceUnderTitleBar`, which puts the banner first (`Top` 0) and moves the strip under the macOS title bar onto it. A failed fetch, pull or push keeps its notice (`TGittanNotice`, with Try Again) and its red status message, not a banner. Hints about a state, such as why Commit is disabled, stay by their control.
- `gittan.textfit.pp` cuts a label to a width with an ellipsis for the self-painted controls (`FitTextRight`, `FitTextLeft` for a path, which keeps its end; `FittingChars` for breaking a line): between UTF-8 characters, never inside one, since a lone lead byte left at the end (a name cut at an "ö") is invalid UTF-8, which Cocoa drew as "?". The file header, the status bar (`Ellipsize`), the banner, the notice, the summary's rows, the popup lists, the Repository page and the welcome view (its paths and `WrapError`) use it; the longest part that fits is found by halving.
- The editor's minimap (`OptMinimapVisible`) is on: it paints the same line backgrounds as the text, so it shows where in the file the changes are. Its tooltip is off, and `OptMinimapCharWidth` keeps it narrow (the default takes a fifth of the editor).
- On macOS the content reaches under the title bar: `gittan.titlebar.pp` (CocoaAll only, used behind `LCLCocoa`) sets `NSFullSizeContentViewWindowMask`, makes the title bar transparent with no separator and returns the strip's height from `contentLayoutRect`; it also turns off `automaticallyAdjustsContentInsets` on the content view (the LCL's `NSScrollView`), which otherwise gets the strip's height as a top inset, so a trackpad scroll over the strip moved the whole form down by it. The LCL makes every windowed control a scroll view of its own (`TCocoaScrollView`, `TCocoaManualScrollHost`), and AppKit gives each that reaches under the strip the same inset; once the content moved into a frame the inset came after the LCL had placed them and scrolled the sidebar and the summary down by the strip, so a category on `TCocoaScrollView` (`TCocoaScrollViewNoTitleInset`) answers no to `automaticallyAdjustsContentInsets` for all of them; the window keeps it (`TitleBarHeight`, 0 on the other widgetsets) and every frame takes it into `FTitleBarHeight` in `Init`, whose `SetupTitleBar` and moves the filter field, the file header, the summary box and the splitter down by it through `BorderSpacing.Top` (`PlaceUnderTitleBar` gives the header's and the summary box's share to the banner while one is shown), so the sidebar's shade runs up behind the traffic lights and the editor's background behind the title, as a sidebar does in Finder or Deezer. The title bar still takes the mouse in the strip (a drag moves the window, a double click zooms it) and no LCL control under it gets a click, so nothing is put there. On macOS 11 and later the window gets a unified `NSToolbar` (`gittan.toolbar.pp`, `TGittanToolbar.Install` from the window's `SetupChrome` in `uwindow.pp`, before the strip is measured, since it makes the strip taller), so the traffic lights sit in a row of a tab bar's height, as in Firefox; its items are native and take clicks. Its one item is the tab strip (`TGittanTabStrip`, an `NSView` of its own that the toolbar draws and takes the mouse of; `setMinSize`/`setMaxSize` let it stretch from the traffic lights to the right edge): a tab per frame with the frame's `TabTitle` (the repository's folder, the one opening, a file open without one, or New Tab) and its `TabHint` (the path) as the tooltip of the tab under the mouse, then a plus. Tabs share the width, `MinTabWidth` to `MaxTabWidth`; the shown one is a filled rounded box (white at 0.13 on a dark window, at 0.85 with a faint border on a light one, told apart by the label color the appearance resolves), a hovered one a fainter box, the others their names in the secondary label color with a faint line between two of them. The cross shows on the shown and the hovered tab, on a round shade while the mouse is on it. A press on a tab selects it (`OnSelect`), a release on the cross or a middle click closes it (`OnClose`), a release on the plus asks for a new tab (`OnNew`); the strip answers no to `mouseDownCanMoveWindow` and moves the window itself from its empty part (`performWindowDragWithEvent`), zooming it on a double click. The window's title is hidden; the window keeps setting its caption (the shown frame's), which Mission Control and the Window menu show. `UpdateTabs` in `uwindow.pp` hands the tabs to the strip whenever a frame is shown, made or closed or its name changes (`FrameChanged`, from the frame's `UpdateCaption`). The filter, the change navigator and the actions belong to a repository and are in its frame: the native `TEdit` over the tree (`FTreeFilter`) and the file header's navigator and pills (`ShowControls`). On Windows and GTK 3 the window draws its own title bar above the frames: `gittan.owntitlebar.pp` holds `TGittanOwnTitleBar`, aligned at the top of the form in place of the caption, which lays out and paints the name and the menus, the filter field (`FTreeFilter`, borderless, in a rounded box with a magnifier and a clear button) centered, the navigator, the header's pills (`HeaderControlsChange`, as on macOS) and the minimize, maximize and close buttons, tells which part a point is in (`PartAt`) and runs the pills, the field and the menu names, and leaves what the window system does to a descendant per widgetset: `Maximized`, `ShowMenu` (the submenu under a name, `MenuClosed` when it has gone), `CaptionButtonClick` and `DrawGlyph` (the buttons' glyphs, the magnifier and the clear cross). `uwindow.pp` makes whichever (`SetupChrome`) and keeps it in `FOwnBar` behind its own `OWNBAR` define (LCLWin32 or LCLGtk3); a frame reaches it through `OwnBar`, nil while the frame is in the background, and no longer gives it the filter field, the navigator or the pills, which the frame shows itself, so the bar has the name, the menus of the frame shown (`Activated` sets the menu and calls `Layout`) and the caption buttons. Its shade runs to the shown frame's `TreeSplitter`'s right edge (`SplitControl`, set by `Activated`, repainted from `TreePanelResize`), its colors come from `ColorSidebar`. On Windows (`gittan.wintitlebar.pp`, behind `LCLWin32`) `InstallTitleBar` (from `SetupChrome`) subclasses the form's window, whose `WM_NCCALCSIZE` keeps the client's top where the window's top is (the frame stays on the other sides, and a maximized window's top is moved in by the frame's thickness), and `TGittanWinTitleBar` draws the glyphs in Segoe Fluent Icons (Segoe MDL2 Assets on Windows 10). The bar's own window answers `WM_NCHITTEST` with `HTTRANSPARENT` outside its menus, search box and pills, so the form answers: the top edge resizes, the caption buttons give their `HT` codes (`HTMAXBUTTON` brings Windows 11's snap layouts) and the rest `HTCAPTION`. The form handles the mouse on the buttons itself (`WM_NCLBUTTONDOWN`/`UP`, `WM_NCMOUSEMOVE`, `WM_NCMOUSELEAVE`), since Windows would paint its old buttons. The menu stays on the window, hidden under the bar, so commands are found in it (LCL looks them up through `GetMenu`) and shortcuts work; a menu name opens its submenu with `TrackPopupMenuEx`, and `SC_KEYMENU` (Alt and a letter) opens the menu with that accelerator instead of the hidden bar. Windows still draws the hidden menu bar, and the line under it (white in dark mode; MetaDarkStyle paints over it at the client's top, now the window's top), over the client area when the window is activated or deactivated: `WM_NCACTIVATE` is passed on with lParam -1 (no repaint), `WM_NCCALCSIZE` notes where the client would have started (`MenuBottom`), and after `WM_NCPAINT` and `WM_NCACTIVATE` `HideMenuBar` has the strip down to there painted again. Built by cross-compiling only (`lazbuild --os=win64 --ws=win32` with a win64 RTL built from the FPC sources); run on Windows 11. On GTK 3 (`gittan.gtktitlebar.pp`, behind `LCLGtk3`) `InstallTitleBar` gives the window an empty box as its title bar (`gtk_window_set_titlebar`; GTK takes it from an unrealized window only, and the LCL realizes the window as it creates it, which `InstallTitleBar` makes it do since the form has no handle yet in `FormCreate` (where the frame's `Init` runs) on gtk3, so the window is unrealized first, not being shown yet, and realized again at once, since `TGtk3WSCustomForm.ShowHide` reads its GdkWindow before showing it), so GTK's client-side decoration under Wayland keeps the shadow, the rounded corners and the resize border in the shadow and only the strip is gone. `TGittanGtkTitleBar` takes the mouse itself, without `csCaptureMouse` (the compositor takes the mouse with a move and the release never comes): a press on an empty part dragged past `DragThreshold` starts the compositor's move (`gtk_window_begin_move_drag`, as GTK's own title bar does), a double click maximizes or restores (`ToggleMaximized`), a right click opens the window menu (`gdk_window_show_window_menu`) and the buttons iconify, maximize and close (`CaptionButtonClick`). The menu stays on the window for its commands and shortcuts, but `HideNativeMenu` (after `BuildMenu`) hides the GTK menu bar's items (with `gtk_widget_set_no_show_all`, since `ShowHide` shows the form with `show_all`) and lets the bar be a pixel high with `gtk_widget_set_size_request` (the LCL subtracts `GetMenuBarHeight` from the form's height, and takes 0 as the menu height of its own); a click on a name runs the item's `OnClick` first (the Branch menu's `BranchMenuOpen`, which the menu bar would run as it opens) and pops the submenu under the name with `gtk_menu_popup_at_widget` shifted by `rect-anchor-dx`, its `deactivate` signal ending the open look. The window's `notify::is-active` sets `Active` and `window-state-event` repaints the maximize glyph (the handlers check `LiveBar`, nil once the bar is gone); the glyphs are lines (`DrawGlyph`). Alt and a letter opens the menu with that accelerator through the window's `OnKeyDown` (`FormKeyDown` in `uwindow.pp`, with `KeyPreview`), as the hidden menu bar's mnemonics would. Compiled on macOS with `make WIDGETSET=gtk3` (everything but the link) and not yet run on Linux. The form's `Height` now counts the strip, as the settings save it. While a repository is indexed, `UpdateIndexBand` (from `UpdateStatus`, and from `IndexNext` for every file, since the big files of a large repository take seconds between the status bar's updates every `IndexReportEvery` files) shows the progress as a band along the top edge over the title bar, above the traffic lights (`ShowTitleBand`, `HideTitleBand` in `gittan.titlebar.pp`, in `FBandTrack` and `FBandFill` from `ApplyTheme`; the fill is `MinFill` wide at least, since the window's rounded corner hides the first pixels): two `NSBox`es, a track and a fill, in the window's frame view above the content view, since a subview of the content view (the LCL's scroll view) ends under its content, and an LCL control a few pixels high is not drawn by Cocoa at all (a `TCustomControl` under about 25 px showed nothing, a taller one filled its bounds with its color). On Windows and GTK 3 `UpdateIndexBand` hands the same progress to `TGittanOwnTitleBar.SetBand`, which paints the band along the top of the self-drawn bar (`BandHeight`, the fill `MinBandFill` wide at least for the rounded corner of Windows 11 and GNOME) and invalidates only the band's strip, and only when the fill grows by a pixel. Go > Filter Files (Cmd+Shift+F) focuses the field; `FillFileTree` keeps only the files whose path holds the field's text in lower case (`FTreeFilterText`), and `TreeFilterChange` refills the tree on every change and reselects the shown file with the selection handler off, so it is not reloaded. Escape clears the field, or leaves it when it is empty; Enter and Down go to the tree. The summary and the counts are not filtered.
- `gittan.changenav.pp` holds `TGittanChangeNav`, a pill at the right end of the file header with a previous and a next arrow and a counter ("2 / 5", or "– / 5" before the caret has reached a change). It paints itself with `TCanvas` primitives in colors `ApplyTheme` sets through `SetColors`, because a native push button keeps its light bezel on a dark background on macOS (the bezel takes the window's white background) while a caption follows the theme's text color and turned white on white. The header anchors it to its right edge and centers it vertically, so it stays in place when it grows with its counter; `TGittanFileHeader.SetChanges` shows or hides it and repaints the header, whose room for the path depends on the pill's width.
